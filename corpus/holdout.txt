A natural history museum is preparing a new exhibition about the relationship between oceans and the atmosphere. The curators begin with the water cycle. Water evaporates from the surface, condenses into clouds, and returns as precipitation. These familiar processes transfer energy as well as water. The explanation must distinguish evaporation from boiling and emphasize the importance of temperature, wind, and humidity. A visitor asks why the temperature near a coast sometimes changes more slowly than the temperature far inland. The curator explains the large heat capacity of water and the complex interactions between land, ocean currents, and prevailing winds.

Suppose a routing table contains three candidate paths to the same destination. Each path has a cost, a next-hop address, and an administrative preference. If the preferred path becomes unavailable, a network device may select a different route according to its configuration. That transition can produce packet loss until routing information converges. Troubleshooting should separate forwarding-plane symptoms from control-plane causes. It is not sufficient to ping one endpoint and infer that every application is reachable. A path can carry small packets correctly while larger packets fail because of mismatched maximum transmission units or fragmentation behavior.

An economic report compares two hypothetical neighborhoods. One neighborhood has a rising population but few new homes, and the other has a stable population with substantial construction activity. Prices may differ for multiple reasons, including access to employment, financing costs, local regulations, and residents' expectations. It would be misleading to attribute a price difference to a single factor without examining the others. When reporting a correlation, the analyst should identify the data source, the period covered, the selection criteria, and important uncertainties.

A programmer writes a function that calculates the greatest common divisor of two positive integers. The first version repeatedly subtracts the smaller number from the larger. A later version uses the remainder operation and needs fewer iterations for large numbers. Both versions should agree on representative input pairs. If a test accidentally compares the result with itself, it provides no independent evidence of correctness. Good tests include values with a common factor greater than one, values that are relatively prime, and cases where one input divides the other exactly.

A chef decides to change a recipe for bread. One variation uses a higher percentage of water, while another uses a longer fermentation time.
